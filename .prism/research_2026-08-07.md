## Industry practice
- SQL, Python/Pandas, and applied statistics (variance, A/B testing,
  clustering "applied to business scenarios") are the core 2026 data
  analyst screen, with LLM/MLOps questions now showing up regularly even
  at the analyst level, not just data scientist. ([DataQuest](https://www.dataquest.io/blog/data-analyst-skills/), [KORE1](https://www.kore1.com/data-scientist-interview-questions/))
- SQL specifically gets tested on joins, window functions, and query
  optimization — Prism's SQL Lab (DuckDB workbench) already covers this
  ground; nothing new needed there this cycle. ([LockedInAI](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/data-analyst-interview-questions-prepare-guide))

## Competitor tools
- Julius AI: natural-language-to-chart, upload-and-ask conversational
  analysis — closest analog to Prism's AI Analyst + Auto Analyst combo.
- Hex / Deepnote: notebook-first, SQL+Python, team collaboration — not
  Prism's lane (single-user portfolio tool), but their "structured
  reproducible workspace" framing is worth noting for any future
  session-replay/export polish.
- None of the surveyed tools foreground **statistical rigor across many
  hypotheses at once** (multiple-comparisons correction) as a headline
  feature — they lean on single ad hoc LLM-generated charts/explanations
  instead. That's a real, differentiated gap Prism can own. ([Deepnote comparisons](https://deepnote.com/compare/juliusai-vs-deepnote), [Julius AI tools survey](https://julius.ai/articles/ai-tools-for-data-analysis))

## Ecosystem tech
- statsmodels (already a Prism dependency, previously only used elsewhere
  in the codebase) has a ready-made, well-tested Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
  implementation (`statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests`) — no new
  dependency needed for hypothesis correction.
- polars/DuckDB adoption trend noted but not actioned: Prism's SQL Lab
  already runs on DuckDB; a polars migration for the pandas core would be
  an architecture change, explicitly out of scope per the guardrails.

## Agentic EDA research
- Current LLM-based data-analysis agents (Code Interpreter-style, Pandas
  Agent, InsightPilot) mostly generate descriptive insights from a single
  pass; multi-agent "debate"/self-verification designs (e.g. DataSage) are
  an active research direction but heavy (multi-role debating, external
  knowledge retrieval) — too much machinery for a free-tier Gemini budget.
  A lighter, still-rigorous self-check that fits Prism's constraints:
  statistical correction as a built-in verification step, rather than a
  second LLM pass re-checking the first one's claims. ([arXiv 2511.14299 DataSage](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14299), [arXiv 2510.04023 LLM data science agents survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04023))
- Gartner-cited trend: by 2028, 60% of self-service analytics users will
  use general-purpose LLMs for ad hoc/exploratory analysis — reinforces
  investing further in Prism's agentic analysis surface (Auto Analyst,
  Atlas) rather than treating it as done.
