# Prism Research — 2026-08-07

Web sweep across industry practice, competitor tools, ecosystem tech, and
agentic-EDA research, synthesized against `.prism/audit_2026-08-07.md`'s
findings on what Prism already has.

## Key findings by source class

**Industry practice.** Rigor around hypothesis testing keeps showing up as
a senior/junior separator in current data-scientist hiring signal — "A/B
testing with rigor (CUPED, sequential, Bayesian)... signals real production
exposure," and "understanding hypothesis testing well enough to explain
your reasoning to someone non-technical" is called out as a hire/no-hire
differentiator. `pandas`/`DuckDB`/`polars` fluency is named as "the single
biggest separator between a junior and a senior Data Scientist."
([techiecv.com](https://www.techiecv.com/resume-skills/data-scientist),
[365datascience.com](https://365datascience.com/career-advice/data-scientist-job-market/))

**Competitor tools.** Julius AI's stats stack (t-tests, chi-square, ANOVA,
correlation, regression, clustering, ARIMA, PCA) is explicitly marketed as
its differentiator for conversational analysis; Hex leans on a code-first
notebook + AI-assist blend; Deepnote's copilot auto-generates whole
notebooks from a prompt. None of the three summaries surfaced a
"propose-then-test" hypothesis workflow as a named, marketed feature —
most tools describe themselves as answering questions asked of them
(reactive), not proposing testable claims unprompted (proactive).
([julius.ai](https://julius.ai/articles/hex-competitors),
[deepnote.com](https://deepnote.com/compare/juliusai-vs-deepnote))

**Ecosystem tech.** No specific new-library signal changed this run's
build decisions — Prism already uses DuckDB (SQL Lab) and scipy/statsmodels
(Stats Lab/Forecasting); polars adoption keeps growing but a
pandas-to-polars migration is an architecture change, out of scope per the
routine's guardrails (logged as a proposal below, not attempted).

**Agentic EDA research.** Current arXiv work (2601-2604 range) is explicit
that verification is the open problem: "DS-STAR" and reward-modeling papers
frame the gap as agents proposing insights without checking them, and one
survey names "trustworthy reporting" as an unsolved challenge for
LLM-driven data-science agents.
([arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24198),
[arxiv.org/html/2510.04023v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04023v1))

That gap — LLM proposes, nothing independently checks — is exactly what
this run's Hypothesis Engine closes for Prism: Gemini proposes, but
`scipy.stats` (not Gemini) delivers the verdict.
