## Notes by source class

**Industry practice** — 2026 job-description scans (AccioJob/DataQuest/etc.)
still center SQL (60%), Excel (81%), Python (41%), Power BI/Tableau, and
"statistical reasoning" as the core ask; polars/DuckDB don't yet show up in
mainstream postings but are a strong differentiator signal in a portfolio,
not a requirement. Prism already covers the SQL/stats/Python/dashboarding
core; the gap is depth signaling (real tests, real verification) more than
breadth.

**Competitor tools** — Julius AI leads on fast NL-to-chart-plus-explanation;
Hex is building team-workflow depth (Magic + Threads) more than analytical
depth; Deepnote leans collaborative-notebook. None of the three publicly
lean into self-verification or adversarial checking of their own AI output
— that's a genuine differentiation lane for Prism, not just a catch-up
feature, which is why hypothesis engine (#1) was prioritized this run.

**Ecosystem tech** — DuckDB is already in Prism (SQL Lab); polars isn't.
The natural extension is using DuckDB (already a dependency) for
large-file profiling instead of pandas `.describe()`/`.value_counts()`
sweeps, which is effort-M, not a new dependency — good next-run pick.

**Agentic EDA research** — the throughline across AutoVerifier, the LLM
data-science-agent survey, and QUIS is the same: automation without
verification is now the *expected baseline*, not a differentiator; critic
loops / adversarial re-checking is where 2026 research has moved.
Hypothesis Engine (#1) and the future Auto-Analyst-critic (#3) are both
direct responses to that shift.
